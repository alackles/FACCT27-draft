
RLHF is a near universally applied \cite{christiano_deep_2017, anthropic_introducing_2023, google_bard_2023, touvron_llama_2023, google_deepmind_gemini_2026, spacexai_introducing_2026} and extensively studied post-training alignment paradigm for modern large language models with known structural limitations; for a detailed history and critique, see \citet{casper_open_2023}. In brief, RLHF incentivizes models towards \textit{producing text which is favorably rated by a human scorer}; this strongly selects for the ability to produce coherent and confident responses, but also orients models structurally towards \textit{evaluator approval} as the primary goal for all reasoning and responses (rather than, for example, factual truth or ethical judgment). This is known to produce failure modes such as replication of human biases \cite{bender_dangers_2021}, misalignment via reward-hacking and reward mis-generalization \cite{skalse_defining_nodate, wang_reward_2026}, and general weakness to a wide variety of exploits and jailbreaks \cite{wei_jailbroken_2023}. 

Although many frontier models are moving away from pure RLHF as a primary training paradigm and towards more scalable, automated methods such as Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback or Constitutional AI \cite{anthropic_system_2026-1, anthropic_system_2026-2, openai_gpt-6_2026, deepseek-ai_deepseek-v41-flash_2026, qwen_team_qwen38-max_2026}, these newer methods of preference-based post-training rely on the earlier corpora of model-responses-and-user-ratings generated by the pure RLHF era of model training and are thus vulnerable to many of the same structural flaws \cite{bai_constitutional_2022, lee_rlaif_2024, srivastava_technical_2026}.
